## INTRODUCTION

Discipline is one of the least enjoyable, but one of the most critical aspects of labour relations. It is essential for effective service delivery to have a disciplined workforce. Equally important for service delivery is the ability to effectively deal with incapacity and ill-health problems in the work situation.

### Transformation of Public Service Discipline

Discipline in the public service was transformed with effect from 1 July 1999. The transformation was done within the ambit of the new management framework that is based on the devolution of power to national and provincial departments.

### Public Service Co-ordinating Bargaining Council (PSCBC) Agreements

The transformation culminated in three key agreements:

- **Disciplinary Code and Procedures (Resolution 2/99)**
  - Implemented: 1 July 1999
- **Incapacity Code and Procedures for Poor Performance (Resolution 10/99)**
  - Effective: 1 July 1999
- **Incapacity Code and Procedures for Ill-Health (Resolution 12/99)**
  - Effective: 3 December 1999

### Key Changes

The new Codes address problems associated with the application of disciplinary and incapacity measures under the Public Service Act, 1994.

**Major shift:** From statutory prescription to collective agreement, which:
- Lends legitimacy to the Codes
- Ensures co-ownership between social partners

## PART A: DISCIPLINARY MATTERS

### KEY POINTS: DISCIPLINE

When applying discipline, always keep the following in mind:

#### Adherence to Principles and Procedures

Core principles:
- Follow the principles of the Code
- Apply discipline as soon as you become aware of the infraction
- Do not discipline in anger
- Never dismiss an employee before a disciplinary enquiry has been held
- Explore all actions short of dismissal before considering dismissal
- Gather all facts before deciding to institute disciplinary action
- Follow correct procedure and ensure proper reason exists

#### Trade Union Representatives

If the employee to be disciplined is a union representative, follow the requirements expressed in Item 4.2 of Schedule 8 of the Labour Relations Act, 1995 (LRA)

#### Natural Justice

Always adhere to the rules of natural justice:

**Informed decision-making**
- Before taking a decision, fully inform the employee of the case against them
- Allow employee proper opportunity to present their case

**Impartiality**
- If you have a personal interest in the outcome, do not chair proceedings (applies to disciplinary enquiries)

**Perceived fairness**
- Justice must not only be done but must be seen to be done
- No bias should be evident in dealings with hearings

---

### WHAT DISCIPLINARY ACTION TO TAKE

#### Transgressions

**Definition:** Before instituting disciplinary action, you must be convinced that a transgression took place.

**List of transgressions:** Appears in the Annexure to the Disciplinary Code

**Important notes:**
- The list is not exhaustive
- Employees may be disciplined for other conduct if they know, or ought to have known, that the conduct constitutes grounds for disciplinary action

#### Becoming Aware of Transgressions

You will become aware of a transgression in one of the following ways:
- By yourself (seeing, hearing, etc.)
- Through an allegation of misconduct by somebody else

#### Options for Dealing with Transgressions

The Code provides the following options:
- Counselling the employee
- Utilising a system of warnings (progressive discipline)
- Holding a disciplinary enquiry

#### Deciding on the Option

When deciding which option to use:

**Step 1: Investigate**
- Investigate the allegations of misconduct

**Step 2: Assess seriousness**

Consider:
- The actual or potential impact of the alleged misconduct on:
  - The work of the public service
  - The employee's component and colleagues
  - The public
- The nature of the employee's work and responsibilities
- The circumstances in which the alleged misconduct took place

---

### PROGRESSIVE DISCIPLINARY ACTION

#### Progressive Discipline Philosophy

**Key principle:** Do not rush into a disciplinary enquiry every time an employee transgresses.

**Benefits:**
- Progressive disciplinary action may yield desired results
- Builds harmonious relationships

#### Available Progressive Measures

- Corrective counselling
- Verbal warnings
- Written warnings
- Final written warnings

#### Not Mechanistic Application

**Important:** The corrective steps may be applied progressively, but should not be applied mechanistically.

**What this means:**
- Steps do not necessarily have to build on one another
- Depending on seriousness of transgression, you might employ:
  - A written warning in the first instance, OR
  - A final written warning in the first instance

---

### CORRECTIVE COUNSELLING

#### When to Apply

**Purpose:** To probe causes of misconduct together with the employee and jointly identify means of correcting unacceptable conduct.

**Appropriate situations:**
- Cases of less serious nature
- Where there is room for change and improvement
- Where a trend has developed (e.g., late coming)
- Generally not used in once-off situations

#### Counselling Steps

**Step 1: Invite the employee**
- Invite to your office
- Inform employee timeously what the meeting is about

**Step 2: Ensure privacy**
- Ensure absolute privacy for the meeting
- Ensure no interruptions

**Step 3: Identify the problem**
- Draw employee's attention to alleged misconduct
- Ensure both agree that it occurred or is occurring

**Step 4: Understand reasons**
- Determine reasons for misconduct
- Ask employee to explain their conduct

**Step 5: Seek solutions**
- Request employee to suggest ways of correcting behaviour
- Offer your suggestions
- Come to consensus position on how to deal with conduct

**Step 6: Agree on action plan**
- Agree on each party's role
- Set a return date for assessment of progress

**Step 7: Document**
- Keep a note of the meeting
- Especially record the agreement reached

**Step 8: Follow up**
- On return date, check progress
- Assess whether employee co-operated
- If employee did not co-operate, consider further disciplinary steps (warnings)

#### Important Notes

**Note 1:** If employee or union representative refuses to acknowledge transgression:
- Terminate counselling meeting
- Consider other disciplinary steps

**Note 2:** If during counselling transgression appears more serious than initially thought:
- Terminate meeting
- Apply other disciplinary steps available

---

### WARNINGS

#### When to Apply

Use warnings when you are of the opinion that the transgression:
- Requires more serious action than mere counselling
- BUT is less serious than a transgression requiring a disciplinary enquiry

#### Steps for Issuing a Warning

**Step 1: Invite to meeting**
- Invite employee (and representative if required) by written notice
- See Annexure A for example

**Step 2: Hold meeting**
- Hold in private
- Keep as informal as possible

**Step 3: Present allegation**
- Recap alleged misconduct
- Allow employee opportunity to state their side

**Step 4: Decide**
- Consider employee's response
- Decide on sanction to be applied

**Step 5: Convey decision**
- At conclusion of meeting, OR
- Afterwards if you need more time to consider
- See Annexures B and C for examples

#### Severity of Warnings

**Hierarchy (least to most severe):**
- Verbal warning (least severe)
- Written warning
- Final written warning (most severe)

**Factors influencing type:**
- Alleged misconduct
- Employee's response to allegation
- Other valid warnings for same or similar offence (act as aggravating factors)

#### Validity Periods

- **Placement and Removal**
  - Written warnings:
    - Must be filed on employee's personal file
    - Must be removed and destroyed at expiry of validity period

#### Important Notes

**Note 1:** Allow employee sufficient time to prepare for meeting

**Note 2:** If employee fails/refuses to attend without valid reason:
- Go ahead and issue warning you regard as appropriate

**Note 3:** If employee fails to attend but sends representative:
- Can continue if representative has written mandate from employee
- If no mandate, continue as in Note 2

**Note 4:** Do not type warning before meeting:
- This means you've already decided outcome before hearing other side

**Note 5:** Keep record of warnings issued:
- Date issued
- Transgression
- Type of warning
- Although actual warnings removed at expiry, record may be used if employee later commits same/similar offence and claims to be first offender

### DISCIPLINARY ENQUIRY

#### When to Apply

Ask the following questions to determine if disciplinary enquiry is appropriate:
- Is transgression of such serious nature that counselling or warning will not suffice?
- Does transgression constitute material breach of employment relationship?
- Has employee repeated transgression or committed related transgression during validity of warning (especially final written warning)?
- Could dismissal be an appropriate sanction?

**If answer to any question is YES:** Consider disciplinary enquiry

#### Steps for Disciplinary Enquiry

**Step 1: Appoint representative of employer**
- Should, as far as possible, be manager of the employee
- Must be an officer in the public service

**Step 2: Conduct investigation**
- Conduct thorough investigation into allegation
- Advisable to be conducted by same person who will represent employer at hearing

**Step 3: Assess evidence**
- Ensure enough evidence to sustain allegation before giving notice

**Step 4: Appoint chairperson**
- Must be officer of higher grade than employer's representative
- **Exception for head of department:** May be person from outside public service if Cabinet or Provincial EXCO approves

**Step 5: Notify employee**
- Inform employee at least 5 working days before hearing
- See Annexure D for example

**Step 6: Hold hearing**
- Must be held within 10 working days of notifying employee

---

### SUSPENSION

#### Forms of Suspension

For purposes of disciplinary enquiry, suspension may take either form:
- Suspension on full pay
- Transfer of employee (to another section/workplace)

#### Requirements for Suspension

Both elements must be present:
- Employee must have allegedly committed serious offence
- Employee's presence at workplace might:
  - Jeopardise investigation into alleged misconduct, OR
  - Endanger well-being or safety of any person or state property

#### Suspension Documentation

- **Annexure E:** Notification to attend meeting where possible suspension will be considered
- **Annexure F:** Letter of suspension

#### Timeframes

Once employee suspended:
- Disciplinary hearing must be held within 1 month
- Only chairperson may postpone hearing (after hearing arguments from parties)
- Postponement entails continued suspension

---

### DECISION AND SANCTION

#### Finding

At conclusion of hearing, chairperson will make finding:
- Guilty, OR
- Not guilty

#### Sanctions (if found guilty)

Chairperson may pronounce any of the following:
- Counselling
- Written warning
- Final written warning
- Suspension without pay (maximum 3 months)
- Demotion
- Combination of above
- Dismissal

#### Special Sanctions

Suspension without pay and demotion:
- May only be applied as alternative to dismissal
- Require employee's agreement
- If agreement cannot be obtained, dismissal will follow

#### Communication

Sanction must be conveyed to employee within 5 working days of hearing

---

### APPEAL

#### Right to Appeal

Employee found guilty may appeal:
- The finding, OR
- The sanction, OR
- Both

#### Appeal Provisions

**Implementation suspension:**
- No sanction may be implemented if employee appeals

**Appeal authority:**
- For employees below head of department level: Executing authority
- For heads of provincial departments: Relevant Premier
- For heads of national departments: The President

**Delegation:** Executing authority may appoint officer in public service as appeal authority provided:
- Officer was not involved in decision to institute disciplinary proceedings
- Officer has higher grade than chair of disciplinary hearing

**Timeframe:**
- Employee must appeal within 5 working days of receiving notice of final outcome

**Process:**
- Normally considered on documents only
- May be rehearing if there was gross procedural irregularity

**Appeal decisions:** Appeal authority may:
- Uphold appeal
- Reduce sanction
- Confirm outcome of disciplinary proceeding

**Implementation:**
- Decision of appeal authority shall be implemented

**Further dispute resolution:**
- Employee still has right to declare dispute if not satisfied with appeal outcome
- Disputes dealt with through dispute resolution mechanisms of relevant sectoral bargaining council

**Appeal Example:** See Annexure G for notice of appeal example

#### Important Notes

**Note 1:** Departments should establish departmental policy indicating who decides on appointment of:
- Representative of employer
- Chairperson for disciplinary hearings

**Note 2:** Employees may also appeal against written and final written warnings issued under progressive discipline system

### FLOW DIAGRAM: DISCIPLINARY PROCEDURES

The disciplinary system has two main parts:

**Part 1: Progressive Discipline System**
- Counselling
- Warnings (verbal, written, final written)

**Part 2: Formal Disciplinary Enquiry**
- Formal hearing process
- Possible suspension
- Appeal process

#### Full Process Flow

```
MANAGER AWARE OF MISCONDUCT
↓
INVESTIGATION BY MANAGER
↓
POSSIBLE ACTION (Choose one path)

PATH 1: Less Serious Misconduct / Progressive Discipline
MEETING
↓
Options:
- No further action
- Counselling
- Warnings:
  * Verbal warning
  * Written warning
  * Final written warning
↓
If warnings ineffective → May lead to disciplinary enquiry

PATH 2: Disciplinary Enquiry (if sanction may be dismissal)
NOTICE (5 working days before hearing)
↓
Optional: SUSPENSION (hearing within 1 month)
↓
HEARING (within 10 working days of notice)
↓
OUTCOME:
- Not guilty → No further action
- Guilty → Sanctions

SANCTIONS (if guilty):
- Counselling
- Written warning
- Final written warning
- Suspension without pay (max 3 months)
- Demotion
- Combination of above
- Dismissal

APPEAL (within 5 working days):
- Appeal upheld → No further action
- Sanction reduced → Implement reduced sanction
- Appeal dismissed → Implement original sanction
```

## PART B: INCAPACITY (POOR PERFORMANCE)

### KEY POINTS: INCAPACITY

When utilising incapacity procedures, keep the following in mind:

#### When to Use Code

**Appropriate situations:**
- Employee unable to consistently perform at expected level
- Trend of poor performance has developed
- NOT for once-off poor performance

**Primary purpose:**
- To improve employee's poor performance
- NOT to rid department of employee

#### Performance Management

**Role of departmental system:**
- Should provide guidelines on when to apply incapacity procedures
- Performance assessments should provide evidence of consistent poor performance

#### Adhere to Procedures

**Critical requirements:**
- Do not dismiss employee on grounds of incapacity before incapacity procedures have been applied
- Ensure provisions of Code are adhered to

#### Applies to Probationers

The Code should also be applied to employees on probation

---

### DECISION TO UTILISE CODE

Before deciding to apply the Code, have clarity on the following:

#### 1. Awareness of Expected Performance

**Question:** Was the employee aware of the level or standard of performance expected of them?

#### 2. Proof

**Questions:**
- Do you have proof that employee in fact under-performed?
- Has poor performance been tracked over period of time?
- Does employee's performance assessment support view that employee is performing poorly?
- Has any other steps been taken (e.g., training courses) to address poor performance?

#### 3. Inability vs. Deliberate Under-performance

**Critical distinction:**
- **Inability:** Employee cannot perform at required standard → Use incapacity procedures
- **Deliberate:** Employee can perform but chooses not to → Use misconduct procedures

#### 4. Assessment Factors (Paragraph 3.2 of Code)

Also assess:
- Impact on public service, component, colleagues, and public
- Extent to which employee fails to meet standard
- Extent to which employee lacks necessary skills to perform job
- Nature of employee's work and responsibilities
- Circumstances of employee

---

### PROCEDURE TO BE FOLLOWED

#### Nature of Incapacity Procedure

**Important:** The incapacity procedure is not a once-off event like a disciplinary hearing, but involves a series of events. It is largely based on the counselling approach.

#### Steps

**STEP 1: Initiate Procedure**

**Actions:**
- Inform employee in writing of reasons necessitating incapacity procedure
- Arrange meeting with employee to discuss matter
- See Annexure A for example

**STEP 2: Counsel Employee**

**Meeting process:**

**2.1 Allow employee to respond**
- Allow employee (or representative) to state position on alleged poor performance

**2.2 Depending on response:**

**If employee agrees to poor performance:**
- Move on to other actions under this step

**If employee denies poor performance:**
- Give employee warning (preferably in writing)
- State that continued poor performance will lead to more serious action in terms of disciplinary procedure

**2.3 If employee acknowledges poor performance:**
- Assess and establish timeframe required for improvement
- Identify appropriate training to be given to employee (if required)
- Agree on ways to deal with external factors affecting performance

**STEP 3: Monitoring**

**During monitoring period:**
- Monitor effect of strategy for improvement throughout identified timeframe

**At end of monitoring period:** Assess whether employee has improved

**If employee remedied poor performance:**
- Inform employee
- Terminate procedure

**If employee failed to remedy poor performance:**
- Give employee written report on outcome of procedure
- Proceed to Step 4

**STEP 4: Follow-up Consultation Meeting**

**Actions:**
- Call employee to meeting (see Annexure B for example)
- This can form part of letter forwarding written report to employee

**Consult with employee by:**
- Explaining outcome of procedure (discuss written report)
- Discussing measures to address problems indicated in report

**STEP 5: Decision**

Following consultation with employee, decide on appropriate action:

**Options:**
- Continue with further counselling cycle (repeat Steps 2, 3, 4, and 5 if required)
- Embark on mentoring programme
- Place employee in more appropriate job
- Dismiss employee

---

### SPECIAL REQUIREMENTS

#### Grant Employee Hearing

Before implementing options of placement in alternative job or dismissal:
- Employee must be given hearing to establish failure to meet requirements
- See Annexure C for example

#### Employee Agreement

**If placement in different job entails lower pay:**
- Employee's agreement must be obtained
- If employee does not agree, dismissal option will probably have to be reconsidered

#### Implementation

Exercise the option decided upon

---

### IMPORTANT NOTES

**Note 1:** Employee may be assisted in all meetings by:
- Co-employee, OR
- Trade union representative

**Note 2:** Advisable to bring witness into meeting where employee is confronted with decision of:
- Placement in alternative job, OR
- Dismissal

**Note 3:** If employee does not agree to accept placement in different job with lower pay:
- Option of dismissal will probably have to be reconsidered

## PART C: INCAPACITY IN RESPECT OF ILL-HEALTH OR INJURY

### KEY POINTS: INCAPACITY - ILL-HEALTH

When utilising ill-health procedure, bear the following in mind:

#### Management Instrument

**Nature:**
- The procedure is a management instrument to be invoked by employer, NOT employee
- Principle to protect employment as far as possible should be adhered to
- NOT a tool employee can use if they don't want to work any longer

#### Direct Link with Ill-Health/Injury

**Appropriate use:**
- Only for poor performance or non-performance directly linked to ill-health or injury

**Inappropriate use:**
- NOT for apparent abuse of sick leave (this constitutes misconduct)
- Only for alcohol/drug abuse where linked to ill-health (i.e., pattern of constant intoxication or drug dependency is clear)
- Otherwise deal with in terms of disciplinary code

#### Medical Evidence

Evidence by medical practitioner is critical in deciding how to deal with employee under this procedure

#### Not Punitive

**Purpose:**
- NOT a tool to punish employee
- Tool to assist employee

---

### WHEN TO UTILISE THE CODE

The incapacity code on ill-health should not be invoked every time an employee takes sick leave. The following factors should guide your decision:

#### 1. Link to Sick Leave

**Questions:**
- Has employee exhausted sick leave for specific sick leave cycle for same or related ailment?
- Has disability leave (under new leave provisions) repeatedly been granted for same or related ailment?

#### 2. Chronic Illness

**Question:**
- Are you aware or do you suspect that employee is suffering from chronic illness?

#### 3. Accident

**Question:**
- Has employee been involved in serious accident?

#### 4. Alcohol/Drug Dependency

**Question:**
- Are you aware or do you suspect that employee is suffering from alcohol or drug dependency?

#### 5. Performance Link

**Question:**
- Has employee's performance suffered as result of illness?

---

### PROCEDURE TO BE FOLLOWED

The Code prescribes the following steps if employee is found not to be performing or performing sub-standard as result of poor health or injury:

#### STEP 1: Investigation

**Conduct investigation to determine:**
- Extent of employee's poor health or injury

**Obtain evidence:**
- Relevant medical evidence on employee's condition
- From employee's medical practitioner or independent medical practitioner

**Allow employee input:**
