# Basic Conditions of Employment Act 75 of 1997

## INTRODUCTION

This Act was published in the Government Gazette on 5 December 1997 and assented to on 26 November 1997.

**Purpose:** To give effect to the right to fair labour practices as stated in Section 23(1) of the Constitution by establishing and regulating basic conditions of employment, and to comply with South Africa's obligations as a member of the International Labour Organisation.

## KEY DEFINITIONS

**Employee:** Any person (excluding independent contractors) who works for another person or the State and receives remuneration, or any person who assists in carrying on the employer's business.

**Employer:** The person or entity for whom an employee works.

**Remuneration:** Any payment in money or in kind made to a person in return for working, including the State as employer.

**Wage:** The amount of money paid for ordinary hours of work, or the hours an employee ordinarily works in a day or week.

**Child:** A person under 18 years of age.

**Week:** A period of seven days within which an employee's working week ordinarily falls.

**Ordinary hours of work:** The hours permitted under Section 9 or any agreement under Sections 11 or 12.

**Overtime:** Time worked beyond ordinary hours of work during a day or week.

---

## PURPOSE OF THIS ACT (Section 2)

The Act advances economic development and social justice by:

- Giving effect to and regulating the right to fair labour practices under Section 23(1) of the Constitution by:
  - Establishing and enforcing basic conditions of employment
  - Regulating variation of basic conditions
- Fulfilling South Africa's obligations as a member state of the International Labour Organisation

---

## APPLICATION (Section 3)

**Applies to:** All employees and employers

**Exceptions:**
- Members of the National Defence Force
- National Intelligence Agency
- South African Secret Service
- Unpaid volunteers working for charitable organizations

**Special provisions:**
- Applies to persons undergoing vocational training (except where regulated by other laws)
- Does not apply to vessel employees covered by Merchant Shipping Act (except as provided in sectoral determinations)

## CHAPTER TWO: WORKING TIME

### Ordinary Hours of Work (Section 9)

**Maximum ordinary hours:**
- 45 hours per week
- 9 hours per day if working 5 days or fewer per week
- 8 hours per day if working more than 5 days per week

**Extensions permitted:**
- Up to 15 minutes per day (maximum 60 minutes per week) to serve members of the public after ordinary hours

**Progressive reduction:** Schedule 1 establishes procedures to reduce maximum hours to 40 hours per week and 8 hours per day over time.

---

### Overtime (Section 10)

**Limits:**
- Maximum 3 hours overtime per day
- Maximum 10 hours overtime per week
- Overtime only by agreement

**Payment:**
- Minimum 1.5 times regular wage for overtime

**Alternatives by agreement:**
- Pay ordinary wage PLUS 30 minutes paid time off for every hour of overtime
- Grant 90 minutes paid time off for each hour of overtime

**Time off provisions:**
- Must be granted within 1 month (or 12 months by written agreement)

---

### Compressed Working Week (Section 11)

By written agreement, employees may work:
- Up to 12 hours per day (including meal breaks) without overtime pay
- Maximum 45 ordinary hours per week
- Maximum 10 hours overtime per week
- Maximum 5 days per week

---

### Meal Intervals (Section 14)

**Requirements:**
- Minimum 1 hour continuous break after 5 hours of continuous work
- Employee must be paid for meal intervals where required to work or be available
- Payment required for any portion exceeding 75 minutes (unless living on premises)

**Variations by written agreement:**
- May reduce to minimum 30 minutes
- May dispense with meal interval for employees working fewer than 6 hours

---

### Rest Periods (Section 15)

**Daily rest:** Minimum 12 consecutive hours between shifts

**Daily rest reduction:** May reduce to 10 hours by written agreement if:
- Employee lives on the premises
- Meal interval lasts at least 3 hours

**Weekly rest:** Minimum 36 consecutive hours, normally including Sunday

**Variations by written agreement:**
- 60 consecutive hours every 2 weeks, OR
- Reduction by up to 8 hours in one week if extended equivalently the following week

---

### Sunday Work (Section 16)

**Payment rates:**
- Double wage for each hour worked on Sunday (if not ordinarily working Sundays)
- 1.5 times wage for each hour worked (if ordinarily working Sundays)

**Minimum payment:** If working less than ordinary shift, must receive at least ordinary daily wage

**Alternative by agreement:** Paid time off equivalent to the payment difference

---

### Night Work (Section 17)

**Definition:** Work performed after 18:00 and before 06:00

**Requirements:**
- Only by agreement
- Employee must be compensated by allowance or reduced working hours
- Transportation must be available between residence and workplace

**Regular night work** (after 23:00 and before 06:00, at least 5 times per month or 50 times per year):

Employer must:
- Inform employee of health and safety hazards
- Inform of right to medical examination
- Provide medical examination (at employer's cost):
  - Before starting or within reasonable period of starting
  - At appropriate intervals while continuing
- Transfer to day work if health condition develops and practicable to do so

---

### Public Holidays (Section 18)

**Work on public holidays:** Only by agreement

**If public holiday falls on ordinary workday:**

**Employee does NOT work:**
- Pay ordinary wage for that day

**Employee DOES work:**
- Pay at least double the ordinary wage, OR
- Pay ordinary wage PLUS amount earned for time worked (whichever is greater)

**If public holiday falls on non-working day:**
- Pay ordinary daily wage PLUS amount earned for work performed

## CHAPTER THREE: LEAVE

### Annual Leave (Section 20)

**Entitlement (choose one method):**

**Method 1:** Minimum 21 consecutive days per 12-month cycle

**Method 2 (by agreement):** 1 day of leave for every 17 days worked or entitled to be paid

**Method 3 (by agreement):** 1 hour of leave for every 17 hours worked or entitled to be paid

**Leave cycle:** 12 months from commencement of employment or completion of prior cycle

**Timing:**
- Must be granted within 6 months after end of leave cycle
- Taken on consecutive days (unless employee agrees otherwise)
- According to agreement between employer and employee, or at employer's determination

**Restrictions:**
- Cannot be taken during other leave periods
- Cannot be taken during notice period (unless employee requests during unpaid leave)
- Cannot work during annual leave
- Cannot be paid instead of taking leave (except on termination)

**Additional provisions:**
- Employer may reduce annual leave by occasional leave granted at employee's request
- Additional paid day if public holiday falls during annual leave on ordinary workday

---

### Pay for Annual Leave (Section 21)

**Payment:** Equivalent to remuneration for working during leave period

**Calculation based on:**
- Rate immediately before leave begins
- In accordance with Section 35

**Timing:** Before leave begins OR on usual pay day (by agreement)

---

### Sick Leave (Section 22)

**Sick leave cycle:** 36 months from commencement of employment or completion of prior cycle

**Entitlement:** Number of days equal to 6 weeks of normal work during cycle

**First 6 months of employment:** 1 day sick leave for every 26 days worked

**Payment:**
- Wage ordinarily received for that day
- On usual pay day

**Reduction by agreement permitted if:**
- Number of sick leave days increased commensurately
- Pay for any day is at least 75% of ordinary wage
- Total pay over cycle at least equivalent to standard entitlement

---

### Proof of Incapacity (Section 23)

**Medical certificate required if:**
- Absent more than 2 consecutive days, OR
- Absent more than 2 occasions during 8-week period

**Certificate must:**
- State employee unable to work for duration of absence due to sickness or injury
- Be issued by medical practitioner or registered professional

**Exception:** Employer cannot withhold payment if employee lives on premises and obtaining certificate not reasonably practicable (unless employer provides reasonable assistance)

---

### Maternity Leave (Section 25)

**Entitlement:** Minimum 4 consecutive months

**Commencement:**
- From 4 weeks before expected birth (unless otherwise agreed), OR
- From date certified necessary by medical practitioner or midwife

**Work prohibition:** Cannot work for 6 weeks after birth (unless certified fit by medical practitioner or midwife)

**Miscarriage/stillbirth:** Entitled to 6 weeks leave after third-trimester miscarriage or stillbirth

**Notification:** Must notify employer in writing (if able):
- At least 4 weeks before commencing leave, OR
- As soon as reasonably practicable

**Payment:** Determined by Minister subject to Unemployment Insurance Act provisions

**Protection:** Cannot require or permit pregnant or nursing employee to perform hazardous work

---

### Family Responsibility Leave (Section 27)

**Applies to employees who:**
- Have been employed longer than 4 months
- Work at least 4 days per week

**Entitlement:** 3 days paid leave per annual leave cycle for:
- Birth of employee's child
- Child's illness
- Death of employee's spouse/life partner
- Death of employee's parent, adoptive parent, grandparent, child, adopted child, grandchild, or sibling

**Payment:**
- Wage ordinarily received for that day
- On usual pay day

**Partial days:** May take for whole or part of day

**Proof:** Employer may require reasonable proof before paying

**Expiry:** Unused entitlement lapses at end of annual leave cycle

**Variation:** Collective agreement may vary number of days and circumstances

---

## CHAPTER FOUR: EMPLOYMENT PARTICULARS AND REMUNERATION

### Written Particulars of Employment (Section 29)

**When:** When employee commences employment

**Must include:**
- Full name and address of employer
- Name and occupation of employee or brief job description
- Place of work (indicate if various places)
- Date employment began
- Ordinary hours and days of work
- Wage or rate and calculation method
- Overtime pay rate
- Other cash payments
- Payments in kind and their value
- Frequency of remuneration payment
- Deductions from remuneration
- Leave entitlements
- Notice period required (or termination date if fixed-term)
- Description of applicable council or sectoral determination
- Previous employment counting toward service
- List of other contract documents and where accessible

**Updates:** Must revise and supply updated particulars when changes occur

**Language:** If employee cannot understand written form, must explain in language employee understands

**Retention:** Keep for 3 years after termination

---

### Keeping of Records (Section 31)

**Minimum required information:**
- Employee's name and occupation
- Time worked by each employee
- Remuneration paid to each employee
- Date of birth (if under 18 years)
- Any other prescribed information

**Retention:** 3 years from last entry

**Prohibition:** No false entries permitted

**Benefit:** Satisfies all employment law record-keeping requirements

---

### Payment of Remuneration (Section 32)

**Currency:** South African currency

**Frequency:** Daily, weekly, fortnightly, or monthly

**Method:** Cash, cheque, or direct deposit into employee-designated account

**Cash/cheque payments:**
- At workplace or agreed place
- During working hours or within 15 minutes of start/end
- In sealed envelope belonging to employee

**Timing:** Not later than 7 days after:
- End of payment period, OR
- Termination of employment (except pension/provident fund payments)

---

### Information About Remuneration (Section 33)

**Payslip must show:**
- Employer's name and address
- Employee's name and occupation
- Payment period
- Remuneration in money
- Amount and purpose of deductions
- Actual amount paid

**If relevant:**
- Remuneration and overtime rates
- Ordinary and overtime hours worked
- Sunday/public holiday hours worked
- Total hours if averaging agreement applies

**Delivery:**
- At workplace or agreed place
- During working hours or within 15 minutes of start/end

---

### Deductions from Remuneration (Section 34)

**Permitted only if:**
- Employee agrees in writing for specified debt, OR
- Required/permitted by law, collective agreement, court order, or arbitration award

**Loss/damage deductions permitted only if:**
- Occurred during employment due to employee's fault
- Fair procedure followed with opportunity to respond
- Total debt does not exceed actual loss/damage
- Deductions do not exceed one-quarter of remuneration in money

**Goods purchased:** Deduction must specify nature and quantity

**Timing:** Amounts deducted for others must be paid to them per agreement/law/order timeframes

**Prohibitions:**
- Cannot require repayment except for overpayment errors
- Cannot require acknowledgment of greater amount than received

---

### Calculation of Remuneration and Wages (Section 35)

**Ordinary work deemed to be:**
- 45 hours per week (unless ordinarily works less)
- 9 hours per day (or 7.5 hours if working more than 5 days per week), unless ordinarily works less

**Monthly calculation:** 4⅓ times weekly remuneration/wage

**Variable remuneration:** Calculate by reference to:
- Preceding 13 weeks, OR
- Shorter period if employed less time

**Annual leave pay, notice pay, and severance pay calculations:**

**Include:**
- Cash value of payments in kind (unless receiving payment in kind)

**Exclude:**
- Gratuities
- Allowances enabling employee to work
- Discretionary payments unrelated to hours or performance

---

## CHAPTER FIVE: TERMINATION OF EMPLOYMENT

### Notice of Termination (Section 37)

**Minimum notice periods:**
- 1 week: Employed 4 weeks or less
- 2 weeks: Employed more than 4 weeks but not more than 1 year
- 4 weeks: Either:
  - Employed 1 year or more, OR
  - Farm worker or domestic worker employed more than 4 weeks

**Provisions:**
- Collective agreement may permit shorter periods
- Employee cannot be required to give longer notice than employer
- Notice must be in writing (except illiterate employees)
- Must be explained orally if employee cannot understand written notice
- Cannot be given during leave periods (Chapter Three)
- Cannot run concurrently with leave (except sick leave)

**Rights preserved:**
- Right to dispute dismissal lawfulness/fairness
- Right to terminate without notice for cause recognized by law

---

### Payment Instead of Notice (Section 38)

**Employer option:** Instead of notice, pay remuneration employee would have received during notice period

**Employer waives notice:** If employee gives notice and employer waives part/all, must pay notice remuneration (unless agreed otherwise)

---

### Employees in Employer-Provided Accommodation (Section 39)

**If employer terminates before lawful date or pays instead of notice:**
- Employer must provide accommodation for:
  - 1 month, OR
  - Until contract could lawfully have been terminated (whichever is longer)

**If employee remains:** Remuneration under Section 38 reduced by agreed accommodation value for period employee remains

---

### Payments on Termination (Section 40)

**Employer must pay for:**
- Unused paid time off owed under Sections 10(3) or 16(3)
- Unused annual leave under Section 20(2)

**If employed longer than 4 months, for incomplete leave cycle:**
- 1 day's remuneration per 17 days worked/entitled to pay, OR
- Any more favorable calculation

---

### Severance Pay (Section 41)

**Definition - Operational requirements:** Economic, technological, structural, or similar needs of employer

**Entitlement:** Employee dismissed for operational requirements receives minimum:
- 1 week's remuneration for each completed year of continuous service

**Calculation:** According to Section 35

**Exceptions:** No severance pay if employee unreasonably refuses alternative employment offer

**Dispute resolution:**
- Refer to council (if jurisdiction exists) or CCMA
- Attempt conciliation
- If unresolved, refer to arbitration
- Labour Court may determine amount when adjudicating dismissal dispute

**Note:** Does not affect other legal payment entitlements

---

### Certificate of Service (Section 42)

**Employee entitled to certificate stating:**
- Employee's full name
- Employer's name and address
- Description of applicable council/sectoral standard
- Employment commencement and termination dates
- Job title or work description at termination
- Remuneration at termination
- Reason for termination (if employee requests)

---

## CHAPTER SIX: CHILDREN AND FORCED LABOUR

### Prohibition of Employment of Children (Section 43)

**Cannot employ child who is:**
- Under 15 years of age, OR
- Under minimum school-leaving age if 15 or older (per South African Schools Act: until last school day of year child turns 15 or completes 9th grade, whichever first)

**Cannot employ child in employment that:**
- Is inappropriate for that age
- Places at risk the child's well-being, education, physical/mental health, or spiritual/moral/social development

**Violation:** Criminal offence

---

### Employment of Children 15 Years or Older (Section 44)

**Minister may regulate:** Subject to Section 43(2), Minister may (on Commission advice) prohibit or set conditions for employing children at least 15 years old and no longer subject to compulsory schooling

**Violation:** Criminal offence

---

### Evidence of Age (Section 47)

**Burden of proof:** If age is relevant factor with insufficient evidence, party alleging compliance must prove it was reasonable to believe (after investigation) person was not below permitted age

---

### Prohibition of Forced Labour (Section 48)

**Prohibition:** All forced labour prohibited (subject to Constitution)

**Offence:** Cannot cause, demand, or impose forced labour for own or another's benefit

**Violation:** Criminal offence

---

## CHAPTER SEVEN: VARIATION OF BASIC CONDITIONS

### Variation by Agreement (Section 49)

**Bargaining council collective agreements** may alter/replace/exclude basic conditions if:
- Consistent with Act's purpose
- Does NOT reduce protection in:
  - Sections 7, 9, and regulations under Section 13 (working time regulation)
  - Section 17(3) and (4) (night work protection)
  - Section 20 annual leave (minimum 2 weeks must remain)
  - Section 25 (maternity leave)
  - Sections 22-24 (sick leave)
  - Chapter Six (children and forced labour)

**Other collective agreements:** May replace/exclude basic conditions to extent permitted by Act or sectoral determination

**Individual agreements:** Employer and employee may agree to replace/exclude to extent permitted by Act or sectoral determination

**Hierarchy:** Cannot have:
- Contract contrary to collective agreement
- Collective agreement contrary to bargaining council collective agreement

---

### Variation by Minister (Section 50)

**Minister may determine replacement/exclusion for:**
- Any category of employees or employers, OR
- Specific employer/employee upon application

**Cannot vary:**
- Sections 7, 9, 17(3)-(4), 43(2), 44, 48
- Regulations under Section 13

**Exception:** May vary Section 43(1) only to allow child employment in advertising, sports, artistic, or cultural activities

**Category variations:**
- Must be on Commission advice
- Issued by Gazette notice

**Individual applications:**
- Minister may request Commission advice or guidelines
- Public service determinations require Public Service and Administration Minister concurrence
- Child employment determinations require Welfare and Population Development Minister consultation
- May be issued if registered trade union consent obtained OR proper notice given to unions and affected employees
- May include conditions and time periods
- May take effect from application date (not before)
- Published in Gazette (organisation applications) or prescribed form (individual employer applications)

**Amendment/withdrawal:** On application by affected party after allowing representations

**Employer obligations:** Must display notice, notify employees, and provide copies to unions/representatives/requesting employees

---

## CHAPTER EIGHT: SECTORAL DETERMINATIONS

### Sectoral Determination (Section 51)

**Purpose:** Establish basic conditions of employment for employees in a sector and area

**Process:** Made by Minister by Gazette notice following Chapter Eight procedures

---

### Investigation (Section 52)

**Before making determination:**
- Minister directs Director-General to investigate conditions in sector/area
- Minister determines terms of reference including:
  - Sector and area
  - Categories/classes of employees
  - Matters to be investigated
- Publish Gazette notice with terms and invite public representations

**Request for investigation:** If employer/employee organisation requests investigation, Minister must either:
- Direct investigation, OR
- Request Commission advice on whether to investigate

---

### Making Sectoral Determination (Section 55)

After Commission report, Minister may make determination covering:
- Minimum terms and conditions (including minimum remuneration rates)
- Adjustment of minimum rates
- Manner, timing, payment conditions
- Prohibition/regulation of payment in kind
- Employment record requirements
- Employee record provision requirements
- Prohibition/regulation of task-based, piecework, homework, contract work
- Minimum housing and sanitation standards (for employees residing on premises)
- Travel and work-related allowances
- Minimum trainee conditions
- Minimum conditions for non-employees
- Training and education schemes
- Pension, provident, medical, sick pay, holiday, unemployment schemes/funds
- Any other remuneration or employment terms/conditions

**May apply to:** All or some employers and employees in sector/area

**Restrictions:**
- Cannot be made for Sections 7, 43(2), 44
- Can only vary Section 43(1) for advertising, sports, artistic, cultural activities
- Cannot reduce protection in Sections 9, 17(3)-(4), or Section 13 regulations
- Cannot publish determination covering:
  - Employees/employers bound by bargaining council collective agreement
  - Matters in sector/area with statutory council collective agreement
  - Matters regulated by sectoral determination in effect less than 12 months

---

### Legal Effect (Section 57)

**Priority:** Where this Act and sectoral determination both regulate same matter, sectoral determination prevails

---

### Employer Obligations (Section 58)

Unless determination provides otherwise, employer must:
- Keep copy available in workplace at all times
- Make available for employee inspection
- Provide copy to:
  - Employee who pays prescribed fee
  - Trade union representative or workplace forum member (free on request)

---

## CHAPTER NINE: EMPLOYMENT CONDITIONS COMMISSION

### Establishment and Functions (Section 59)

**Established:** Employment Conditions Commission

**Functions - Advise Minister on:**
- Sectoral determinations (Chapter Eight)
- Any basic conditions of employment matter
- Any matter arising from Act application
- Government policy effects on employment
- Collective bargaining trends undermining Act's purpose
- (With Welfare Minister) Child employment matters including Section 43 review
- (With Public Service Minister) Public service basic conditions

**Operations:**
- May establish rules for meetings and hearings
- Minister provides necessary staff
- Minister directs research required for Commission functions
- Expenses met by Parliamentary appropriation, subject to Auditor-General audit

---

### Composition (Section 60)

**Members appointed by Minister after NEDLAC consultation:**
- 3 persons knowledgeable about labour market and employment conditions (especially vulnerable/unorganised workers)
- 1 designated as chairperson
- 1 nominated by NEDLAC organised labour
- 1 nominated by NEDLAC organised business

**Requirements:**
- Must be citizens or permanent residents
- Act impartially
- No activities undermining Commission integrity
- Recuse from matters with financial interest or conflict

**Terms:**
- Maximum 3-year terms
- Minister determines remuneration (with Finance Minister concurrence)
- Eligible for reappointment
- May resign in writing
- Minister may remove for serious misconduct, permanent incapacity, or integrity-undermining activities

---

### Public Hearings (Section 61)

Commission may hold public hearings permitting oral representations on matters under consideration

---

### Reports (Section 62)

**Format:** Written reports to Minister

**Considerations (for Sections 59(2)(b)-(e)):** Extent appropriate, consider Section 54(3) factors:
- Employers' ability to carry on business successfully
- Small, medium, micro-enterprise and new enterprise operation
- Cost of living
- Poverty alleviation
- Conditions of employment
- Wage differentials and inequality
- Employment impact of proposed conditions
- Health, safety, welfare impact
- Other relevant information

**Unanimous reports preferred:** If not possible, each member's views reflected

---

## CHAPTER TEN: MONITORING AND ENFORCEMENT

### Labour Inspectors (Section 63)

**Minister may:**
- Appoint public service persons as labour inspectors
- Designate public service persons or bargaining council designated agents to perform inspector functions

**Certificate:** Minister provides signed certificate stating:
- Person is labour inspector
- Which legislation they may monitor/enforce
- Which functions they may perform

---

### Functions (Section 64)

**Labour inspectors may:**
- Advise employees and employers of rights/obligations
- Conduct inspections
- Investigate complaints
- Secure compliance through undertakings or compliance orders
- Perform other prescribed functions

**Conflict of interest:** Cannot perform functions regarding undertakings where inspector has (or may be perceived to have) personal, financial, or similar interest

---

### Powers of Entry (Section 65)

**Without warrant/notice at reasonable times, may enter:**
- Any workplace or business premises (not homes)
- Training premises under Manpower Training Act
- Private employment offices under Guidance and Placement Act

**Homes and other places:** Only with:
- Owner/occupier consent, OR
- Labour Court written authorisation (on sworn/affirmed application stating reasons)

**Notification:** If practical, notify employer and trade union representative of presence and reason

---

### Powers to Question and Inspect (Section 66)

**Labour inspectors may:**
- Require disclosure of information (orally/in writing, alone/with witnesses, under oath/affirmation)
- Inspect and question about records/documents
- Copy or remove records/documents
- Require production/delivery of records/documents to specified place
- Inspect, question about, and remove articles, substances, machinery
- Inspect and question about work performed
- Perform other prescribed functions

**Additional provisions:**
- May be accompanied by interpreter and assistants
- Must produce certificate on request
- Must provide receipt for removed items
- Must return items within reasonable period

**Protections:** Answers to questions cannot be used in criminal proceedings except for perjury or false statement charges

---

### Co-operation (Section 67)

**Requirements:**
- Persons questioned must answer all relevant questions truthfully to best of ability
- Employers and employees must provide reasonable facilities and assistance

---

### Compliance Orders (Section 69)

**When:** If inspector has reasonable grounds to believe employer not complying

**Must set out:**
- Employer name and workplace locations
- Non-complied provisions and conduct details
- Amounts owed to employees
- Any written undertakings and failures to comply
- Required steps (including cessation) and timeframes
- Maximum fine per Schedule Two

**Delivery:** Copy to employer and affected employees (or representative if impractical)

**Display:** Employer must display prominently at workplaces

**Compliance:** Employer must comply within stated time unless objects under Section 71

---

### Limitations (Section 70)

**Cannot issue compliance order if:**
- Collective agreement provides arbitration for disputes about amounts owing
- Employee in category excluded under Section 6(1)(a) or 6(3)
- Proceedings instituted (unless withdrawn)
- Amount payable longer than 12 months

---

### Objections (Section 71)

**Process:**
- Employer may object in writing to Director-General within 21 days
- Director-General may permit late objection if good cause shown
- After considering representations and evidence, Director-General may confirm, modify, or cancel order
- Must specify compliance period for confirmed/modified orders

**Evidence considered:**
- Employer's compliance record
- Whether employer likely aware of provisions
- Steps taken to ensure compliance

**Delivery:** Copy of order to employer and affected employees (or representative)

**Compliance:** Must comply within specified period if confirmed/modified

---

### Appeals (Section 72)

**Process:**
- Appeal to Labour Court within 21 days of Director-General order
- Order suspended pending final determination
- Labour Court may permit late appeal if good cause shown

---

### Labour Court Orders (Section 73)

**Director-General may apply to make Labour Court order** if employer not complied and:
- No objection lodged (for compliance orders), OR
- No appeal lodged (for Director-General orders)

**Deemed arbitration award:** For Labour Relations Act Section 158(1)(c) purposes

---

## CHAPTER ELEVEN: GENERAL PROVISIONS

### Temporary Employment Services (Section 82)

**Employment relationship:** Person procured/provided by temporary employment service is that service's employee

**Exception:** Independent contractors are not employees of temporary employment service

**Joint liability:** Temporary employment service and client jointly and severally liable for non-compliance regarding employees providing services to that client

---

### Duration of Employment (Section 84)

**Counting service:** Previous employment with same employer counts toward employment length if break less than 1 year

**Accounting:** Previous payments or leave granted must be considered when determining current entitlements

---

### Codes of Good Practice (Section 87)

**Minister must issue (after NEDLAC consultation):**
- Code on Arrangement of Working Time
- Code on Protection of Employees during Pregnancy and after Birth

**Minister may:**
- Issue other codes
- Change or replace codes

**Publication:** Gazette

**Effect:** Must be considered when interpreting or applying this Act

---

### Confidentiality (Section 90)

**Offence:** Disclosing information acquired while performing duties under Act relating to another person's financial/business affairs

**Exceptions (disclosure permitted):**
- As required by law
- To enable function performance
- For proper Act administration
- For justice administration
- Information about compliance/non-compliance

**Medical records:** Confidential, available only:
- Per medical ethics
- If required by law/court order
- With employee's written consent

---

### Obstruction, Undue Influence, and Fraud (Section 92)

**Offences:**
- Obstruct or improperly influence function performance
- Obtain prescribed document by fraud, false pretences, or forged document
- Pretend to be labour inspector or function performer
- Refuse/fail to answer lawful questions
- Refuse/fail to comply with lawful requests/orders
- Hinder or obstruct labour inspector or function performer

---

### Penalties (Section 93)

**Jurisdiction:** Magistrates' courts may impose penalties

**Maximum imprisonment terms:**
- Child employment violations (Sections 43, 44, 46): up to 3 years
- Forced labour (Section 48): up to 3 years
- Confidentiality breaches (Section 90): up to 1 year
- Obstruction and fraud (Section 92): up to 1 year

---

### This Act Binds the State (Section 94)

Act binds the State except regarding criminal liability

---

## SCHEDULES

### SCHEDULE ONE: Progressive Reduction of Working Hours

**Goal:** Reduce working hours to 40-hour week and 8-hour day through:
- Collective bargaining and sectoral determinations
- Considering impact on employment, economic efficiency, health, safety, and welfare

**Process:**

**Collective Bargaining:** When party introduces working hours reduction as negotiation topic, parties must negotiate

**Commission Role:** May investigate and recommend on reducing hours in particular sectors/areas

**Department Investigation:** Department of Labour must (after Commission consultation):
- Investigate how to achieve 40-hour week reduction
- Complete report within 18 months of Act commencement
- Submit to Minister

**Monitoring Reports:** Department must (after Commission consultation):
- Monitor and review progress
- Publish progress reports for Minister every 2 years
- Table reports at NEDLAC and Parliament
- Minister may prescribe required returns from employers, unions, councils

---

### SCHEDULE TWO: Maximum Permissible Fines for Non-Compliance

This Schedule sets maximum fines for Chapter Ten non-compliance.

**Fine calculation:** Maximum is amount determined from Table One OR Table Two (whichever applies)

**TABLE ONE: Fines NOT Involving Underpayment**
- First offense: R100 per employee
- Second offense: R200 per employee
- Third offense (or patterns within 12 months/3 years): R300 per employee
- Fourth offense: R400 per employee
- Fifth offense: R500 per employee

**TABLE TWO: Fines INVOLVING Underpayment**
- First offense: 25% of amount due plus interest
- Second offense: 50% of amount due plus interest
- Third offense (or patterns): 75% of amount due plus interest
- Fourth offense: 100% of amount due plus interest
- Fifth or more offenses: 200% of amount due plus interest

**Note:** For underpayment violations, the maximum fine is the GREATER of Table One or Table Two amounts.

### SCHEDULE THREE: Transitional Provisions

**Additional Definitions:**
- **Domestic worker:** Employee defined as "domestic servant" in Basic Conditions of Employment Act, 1983
- **Farm worker:** Employee mainly employed in farming activities (includes domestic workers in farm homes)
- **Mineworker:** Mine employee with hours prescribed under Mine Health and Safety Act regulations
- **Security guard:** Employee defined as "guard" or "security guard" under Basic Conditions of Employment Act, 1983

#### Application to Public Service

This Act (except Section 41 - severance pay) does not apply to public service for 18 months after commencement, unless bargaining council agrees to earlier application.

#### Application to Farm Workers

Sections 6A, 10(2A), and 14(4A) of Basic Conditions of Employment Act, 1983 continue applying to farm workers until regulated by sectoral determination.

Section 17(3) (night work) applies to farm workers who work after 20:00 and before 04:00 at least 5 times per month or 50 times per year (until sectoral determination provides otherwise).

#### Payment in Kind (Domestic Workers and Farm Workers)

"Wage" definition and "payment in kind" regulations from Basic Conditions of Employment Act, 1983 continue applying until sectoral determination regulates these matters.

Minister may amend cash amounts in "payment in kind" definition as if old Act not repealed.

#### Ordinary Hours of Work - Transitional Periods

Farm workers, mineworkers, and security guards may work hours exceeding Section 9(1)-(2) limits per table below, provided:
- Conditions in table complied with
- Does not exceed limits applicable immediately before Act commencement
- No agreement under Sections 11-12 concluded

**Transitional Hours Table:**
- **Farm workers:** Can work up to 48 hours/week for first 12 months
- **Mineworkers:** Can work prescribed Mine Act hours for first 12 months
- **Security guards:** Can work up to 55 hours/week for first 12 months, then 50 hours/week for second 12 months

#### Leave Pay Transition

Annual leave (Section 20(2)) takes effect: On date employee would next have commenced leave cycle under old Act or wage determination

Accrued leave: Any leave earned under old Act but not taken must be added to new Act entitlements

First cycle: Section 20(3) does not apply to leave earned before Act commencement

#### Sick Leave Transition

**Transitional Sick Leave Table:**

This table shows when different categories of existing employees become entitled to new sick leave provisions, based on:
- Their employment status under the old Act
- How long they'd been employed at commencement
- Whether they get the full 6-week entitlement or the first-6-months entitlement

**Deductions:** Sick leave granted per this table may be deducted from Section 22(2) or 22(3) entitlement if:
- Taken before Act commencement, OR
- Taken during period when relevant section was effective

#### Exemptions

Exemptions granted under Basic Conditions of Employment Act, 1983 Section 34 remain in force:
- For period granted, OR
- If indefinite, for 6 months after commencement
- Unless withdrawn by Minister before end of such period

#### Wage Determinations

Wage determinations under Wage Act, 1957 remain in force for their operation period and may be extended/amended as if Wage Act not repealed.

#### Exemptions to Wage Determinations

Exemption licences under Wage Act, 1957 Section 19 remain in force for determination's operation period or until withdrawn, as if Wage Act not repealed.

#### Agreements

**Pre-commencement agreements:** Agreements entered into before commencement that this Act permits remain valid and binding.

**Bargaining council collective agreements:** Provisions in force immediately before commencement remain effective for:
- 6 months after commencement (for provisions covered by Section 49(1)(a)-(d))
- 18 months after commencement (for provisions covered by Section 49(1)(e))
